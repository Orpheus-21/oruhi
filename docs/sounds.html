Phonology

Sounds

Oruhi has twelve sounds. Every syllable is open: it is one vowel, with one consonant before it or none.

Vowels

o/o/The lips are round. This is the holy vowel.

Vowels have no length contrast. The language has no diphthongs. Two vowels side by side stay in two syllables.

Syllables

A syllable is V or CV. A word ends in a vowel. The language has no consonant clusters.

A bare vowel syllable can start a word. A bare o can also end a word: it is the honorific ending -o. No bare vowel stands inside a word.

There are 8 consonants times 4 vowels, which is 32 syllables with a consonant. Four more syllables are the bare vowels. The total is 36.

A root ends in one of nine syllables: pi tu mu nu sa hi ra ru na. No affix ends in one of these syllables. For this reason, a reader finds the end of a root without doubt. The grammar page explains the method.

The holy vowel

A word contains o only if the word is holy. An ordinary word has only a, i, and u. The language has 486 ordinary words and 42 holy words.

The honorific ending -o turns a noun, a pronoun, a verb, or an adjective into a holy word, because the new word contains o. In the ring script, the empty ring marks every holy word, so a reader sees holy words at once.

Stress

Stress falls on the second syllable from the end. A word of one syllable has stress on that syllable. Stress is not written.

Spelling

  • Write each sound with its letter.
  • Write a holy word with a capital first letter, as in Oru.
  • Write the other words in lower case. A sentence does not start with a capital letter.
  • Close a sentence with a period. The language has no other punctuation.
  • Use a hyphen only in teaching, to show the border between a root and an affix.

The question marker ma is a word. It takes the place of a question mark.

Sound and meaning

Oruhi uses sound symbolism for a few words. Three tags force a vowel into the word. The other words take their vowels by chance.
