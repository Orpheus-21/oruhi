Numbers

Numbers

The people of Oruhi count in base 8. They say every digit aloud, and they never skip a zero.

Why base 8

The people count the gaps between their fingers. A gap is empty space, so the unit of counting is a sacred thing. Each hand has four gaps. Two hands have eight.

The word for the gap is Mahuro. It is a holy word.

The eight digits

Zero is the holy word Oru. The other seven digits are ordinary words. They contain no o.

Reading a number

A speaker reads a number from the left, one digit after the other. The language has no words for tens, hundreds, or thousands.

Every digit is spoken, and this includes every zero. Where other languages stay silent for an empty place, the people say Oru. The empty place is the holiest part of the number.

The number 8 has the two digits one and zero, so the speaker says kisa Oru. The number 64 has three digits: kisa Oru Oru.

A long number is a long row of digits. This is a real limit of the language. The check script reads back each number from 0 to 4095 and confirms that the words give the same number.

Counting things

A numeral stands before its noun. The noun stays singular. A numeral takes no affix.
