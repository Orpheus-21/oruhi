Grammar

Grammar

Oruhi puts the verb last and builds every word with suffixes. It has no verb for "to be".

Every example on this page is a real sentence. A script made each Oruhi form from the gloss and then parsed it again, so each sentence has exactly one reading. A line shows the ring script, then the form, the parts, and the gloss. Small capitals are affix labels.

Word order

The order of the clause is subject, object, verb. The language has no prepositions. A case suffix on the noun does the work of a preposition.

A word that describes a noun stands before it. This applies to adjectives, numerals, and owners. An adverb stands before the verb.

No verb for "to be"

To say that A is B, put A before B. An adjective as the predicate takes the tense ending that a verb takes.

Nouns

Oruhi has no articles and no gender. A noun has a number and a case.

The singular has the empty ending. The plural ends in -ka. After a numeral the noun stays singular, because the numeral already shows the number.

Cases

The subject has the empty ending. Each other case has one suffix. A case suffix follows the plural suffix.

Pronouns and question words

A pronoun takes the same suffixes as a noun. The plural of minu is minuka, "we".

Verbs

A verb does not agree with its subject. It has no person and no number.

  • The empty ending means the present, or a timeless fact.
  • -ta means the past. -pa means the future.
  • -nahu makes the verb negative. It follows the tense suffix.
  • -ha makes a command.

Questions

For a question with the answer yes or no, put the particle ma at the end. For any other question, put the question word where the answer goes. Do not add ma.

Joining words and clauses

The particles and, or, and but stand between the two parts that they join. The particles if and because follow the clause that they mark.

Making nouns from verbs

Two suffixes turn a verb or an adjective into a noun. -ki names the act or the state. -ri names the one who does it.

The language has no separate word for "hunter" or "singing". A speaker builds the word from the verb. The new noun takes the plural and the case suffixes.

The priests use this rule. A Keeper is a "one who guards". The owner stands before the noun, so "Keeper of the Empty" has the order zero, then guard.

The holy register

The honorific suffix -o goes last on a word. It honors the thing that the word names, or it addresses the holy. A word with -o contains the vowel o, so it is holy.

The empty ending is the holiest form. Prayers and holy statements keep it. The sentence below states a holy truth, so its verb has no tense suffix.

How a reader finds the parts of a word

The reader works from the right end of the word. The reader removes one affix at a time until the rest is a root in the lexicon. The affix tables must also fit the part of speech of the root.

The method gives one answer for every word because of two design rules:

  • No root ends in the last syllable of an affix. The one exception is nahu, "nothing", which has the same form as the affix -nahu.
  • No two affixes end in the same syllable.

The check script tests this claim. It builds every root with every legal row of affixes and parses each result. Each word must have exactly one reading.

What this grammar does not cover

The grammar has no rule yet for these things: clauses inside clauses (such as "the man who came"), comparison ("bigger than"), the passive voice, and exact rules for a list of more than two items. The sample texts avoid them.
